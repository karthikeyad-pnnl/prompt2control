## Section 3 — General Comments and Recommendations

| Original Text with Issue | Suggested Edit | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| The introductory paragraph is concise but does not state *what* the block outputs or controls (heat pumps, pumps, setpoints, bypass valves). A reader cannot quickly understand the scope without reading the full table. | Expand the intro to one additional sentence, e.g., "It generates enable commands for heat pumps and pumps, supply temperature and differential pressure setpoints, and minimum flow bypass valve commands." | Rule 4 requires a short but complete introduction. For a top-level plant controller with >40 output signals, stating the control scope in the intro greatly aids comprehension. |
| The section heading `<h4>Details</h4>` is non-standard and mixes three distinct topics: (1) `staEqu` parameter documentation, (2) `pumHeaWatPri*` naming convention, (3) fault-handling limitation. | Reorganise into `<h4>Assumptions and limitations</h4>` (fault handling) and `<h4>Implementation</h4>` (staEqu and naming convention), as noted in Section 1. | Separating concerns into the prescribed headings improves scanability and compliance with Rule 4. |
| Long table cell notes (e.g., the "Type of distribution" row) use inline `<br/>` to approximate paragraphs, making the HTML harder to maintain and inconsistent with the rest of the library. | Replace `<br/>\n` paragraph breaks inside `<td>` with `<p>…</p>` blocks. | Rule 3 mandates `<p>…</p>` for body text. Consistent use improves rendering across tools. |
| The ASHRAE reference is formatted as a plain `<li>` text entry.
